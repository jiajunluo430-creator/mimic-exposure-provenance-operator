# Journal selection decision

Decision date: 2026-08-05  
Final decision: **GO_JBI**  
Selected target: **Journal of Biomedical Informatics, Research Paper**  
Backup: **International Journal of Medical Informatics, Original Work**

## Why JBI now passes the frozen gate

The rejected JAMIA manuscript was an audit with a conceptual framework. The upgraded project now delivers a versioned machine-readable operator `O=(S,I,T,E,M)`, an installable reference implementation, a unified CLI, adapter-aware compilation, four fail-closed terminal states, privacy-safe provenance reports, and executable comparison tooling. Exact native parity passed 19/19 checks. Prespecified ablations demonstrated information loss and structural non-measurability. A bounded comparison covered eight comparator families and nine dimensions without a superiority score. Execution extended beyond native MIMIC-IV 3.1 through matched public native/FHIR demonstrations, an OMOP semantic-loss experiment, and a full eICU interface-semantic comparison. Software validation passed 10/10 gates with 30 tests.

These observed outputs satisfy the pre-frozen JBI requirements for innovative method development, generalizability, assessment of applicability, and implementation evidence. MIMIC-IV remains the exact-parity reference environment; it is not the title-level object of the new manuscript.

## Why the alternatives were not selected first

- **IJMI** is the first backup and has excellent interoperability/system-evaluation fit, but its 3000-word Original Work limit would force substantial compression of the formal specification and multi-representation evaluation.
- **JAMIA Open** is appropriate for practical public software, but the completed method and transport evidence now meet the stronger JBI hierarchy.
- **BMC Medical Informatics and Decision Making** is a credible open-software backup, but it ranks below JBI/IJMI under the frozen evidence hierarchy.
- **PDS** remains the safest measurement-method fallback if informatics editors do not accept the software contribution; it would require a deliberate, non-cosmetic return to the pharmacoepidemiologic measurement story.

## Claim boundary for the JBI manuscript

The manuscript may claim a machine-executable provenance operator and bounded portability across evaluated representations. It may not claim universal superiority, biological exposure, full-release FHIR validation, OMOP clinical validation, eICU external validation, or drug efficacy/safety. The 40-study validator is a reproducible calculation over human-coded inputs and remains limited by single coding.
